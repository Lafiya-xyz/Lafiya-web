## Title
Add access observability and optional time-boxed sharing scopes to the public emergency card, without adding authentication friction to the offline/no-login responder path

## Difficulty
10/10 — Expert. Estimated effort: 5–7 days for a senior engineer.

## Context
`app/(public)/card/[id]/page.tsx` and `supabase/migrations/20260709110953_emergency_card_rpc.sql`'s `get_emergency_card(p_card_id uuid)` together implement the entire access-control model for a patient's emergency medical data: possession of an unguessable 122-bit random UUID (`card_public_id`). This is a deliberate, documented design choice — the README is explicit that a responder must be able to scan a QR code with "no login" — and it is the right call for the emergency use case. But it has a consequence the codebase has not addressed at all: **once a `card_public_id` is known to anyone (found on a lost printed card, screenshotted, intercepted, guessed via the low-entropy record-hash correlation described in the related record-hash issue in this batch, or simply retained by someone the patient no longer trusts), that party has permanent, unlogged, unaudited, unlimited read access to the patient's blood group, genotype, allergies, medications, chronic conditions, and emergency contacts** — forever, or until the patient discovers the exposure and rotates the ID (itself an incomplete revocation mechanism, per the related card-rotation-cache issue in this batch).

There is no table anywhere in `supabase/migrations/` that records a single access to a card. `get_emergency_card` is a pure, side-effect-free `stable` SQL function (`supabase/migrations/20260709110953_emergency_card_rpc.sql:26-27`) by design — it does not, and per its `stable` marking should not, write anything. `app/(public)/card/[id]/page.tsx` logs nothing about who viewed a card or when beyond a generic `console.error` on failure paths. A patient who suspects their card has been improperly accessed — a real, foreseeable scenario for domestic-violence, custody-dispute, or workplace-conflict contexts where an emergency contact list and health status are exactly the kind of information someone might seek out against the patient's wishes — has **no way to find out**, and no way to issue a link that expires after a single use or a bounded time window for situations where they want to share their card with, say, a specific traveling companion or event, without permanently exposing it.

## Problem statement
Design and implement (1) a privacy-respecting access log for the public card endpoint that a patient can view from their own authenticated profile, and (2) an optional, additional access token scheme that lets a patient generate a time-boxed or view-count-limited sharing link for their card, distinct from their permanent `card_public_id`, that a responder can still use with zero login and zero added latency on the common case — all without turning the emergency page into something that requires a network round-trip to a possibly-unavailable service before rendering (it must still work if the audit-logging write fails or is slow; logging must never block or break access to emergency data).

## Current behavior
- `supabase/migrations/20260709110953_emergency_card_rpc.sql` — `get_emergency_card` is a pure `stable` function; no access is ever recorded anywhere.
- `app/(public)/card/[id]/page.tsx` — no logging of successful views at all (only `console.error` on failure paths, and even that omits any viewer-identifying information by design, consistent with `lib/logging/logger.ts`'s redaction rules).
- `lib/supabase/types.ts` — no `card_access_log` (or equivalent) table/type exists.
- There is exactly one identifier for accessing a card (`card_public_id`) with exactly one lifetime (permanent, until manually rotated) and exactly one scope (full emergency data, unlimited views) — no narrower-scoped alternative exists anywhere in the schema or app.

## Required behavior
- A new, RLS-protected table recording card views, populated by *something* in the read path (the RPC's `stable` marking and side-effect-free design should be preserved for the actual data lookup — you must decide where the logging write belongs: a separate call from the Server Component after a successful RPC read, a wrapping non-`stable` function, or an async/best-effort mechanism — and justify your choice, particularly regarding what happens if the logging write fails or is slow, which must never delay or break the emergency-data response itself).
- The access log must record enough to be useful to the patient (approximate time; a coarse, non-identifying signal such as country-level geolocation from an IP if you choose to include it, or simply timestamp + count — justify what you include, and explicitly exclude anything that would let the patient deanonymize a specific viewer in a way that creates a *new* privacy risk in the other direction) without logging anything from the redacted-fields list in `lib/logging/logger.ts` (no patient health data ever belongs in this log, only meta-access-events).
- The patient's authenticated profile page must be able to display "your card was viewed N times in the last 30 days" (or similar) sourced from this log, scoped by RLS to their own `user_id` only.
- A new, optional token/link type — distinct from `card_public_id` — that a patient can generate from their profile, scoped to a maximum number of views and/or an expiry timestamp, that resolves to the same emergency data via a comparably fast, still-login-free path, and stops resolving once its view count or expiry is exhausted (with a clear, honest "this link has expired" response, not a generic 404, since a genuine responder in an emergency deserves to know *why* the card isn't showing rather than assume it doesn't exist).
- Both the permanent `card_public_id` path and the new scoped-token path must continue to satisfy the existing offline service-worker caching story (a scoped link that expired must not be servable from a stale offline cache as if still valid — coordinate with, but do not duplicate, the cache-revocation mechanism from the related card-rotation issue in this batch).

## Constraints
- Must not add a synchronous network dependency or meaningful latency to the common-case, no-login card view — logging must be fire-and-forget/best-effort from the perspective of the emergency-data response.
- Must not let the access log itself become a new privacy leak (e.g., do not store anything that lets the patient or an attacker who compromises the patient's account single out and identify a specific real-world viewer — this must remain a coarse, aggregate signal, not a surveillance tool).
- The scoped-sharing-token feature must not become a second, weaker access-control bypass of the record-hash/attestation concerns raised elsewhere in this batch — a scoped token must be at least as unguessable as `card_public_id` itself (do not derive it from anything low-entropy).
- Do not rely on repository snapshots or point-in-time repo states; work against the live default branch only.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] A new migration adds an access-log table (or equivalent), RLS-scoped so only the owning patient can read their own card's access history, with no insert/update policy for `authenticated`/`anon` roles (writes happen via a service-role or `security definer` path, matching this repo's existing pattern for privileged writes).
- [ ] A test proves that a failure or artificial delay in the access-logging write path does not delay or break the emergency-card response (e.g., mock the logging call to reject/hang and assert the card still renders correctly and promptly).
- [ ] A test proves the patient's profile can retrieve an aggregate view count/recent-access summary for their own card, scoped correctly by RLS (another user's access log must not be readable, following the existing pattern in `tests/integration/rls.test.ts`).
- [ ] A new migration and RPC (or equivalent) implement the scoped/time-boxed sharing token, with tests proving: a token resolves correctly while within its view-count/expiry budget, stops resolving once exhausted with a distinguishable "expired" response, and cannot be guessed/brute-forced faster than `card_public_id` itself (state the entropy and justify it).
- [ ] A test proves the scoped token, once expired, is not servable from the offline service-worker cache as if still valid (or, if you determine full enforcement there is infeasible per the related cache-revocation issue's findings, this is explicitly documented as a known, bounded residual risk rather than silently ignored).
- [ ] `npm run test:integration` passes against a local Supabase instance with the new migrations applied.
- [ ] `npm run typecheck` and `npm run lint` pass.

## Out of scope
- The card-ID-rotation cache-revocation mechanism itself (separate issue in this batch) — you may depend on its existence for the scoped-token expiry-vs-offline-cache interaction, but you do not need to re-solve it; note the dependency explicitly if your design relies on it, and make this issue's core (access logging + basic scoped tokens) workable even if that issue hasn't landed.
- Any UI/notification feature beyond a simple aggregate count on the profile page (e.g., real-time "someone just viewed your card" push notifications) — out of scope; the profile-page summary is sufficient.
- The record-hash/preimage privacy issue elsewhere in this batch — this issue assumes `card_public_id`/the new scoped token are the access surface, not the record hash.

## Hints and references
- Privacy-preserving access-logging patterns (coarse aggregation, k-anonymity-style thresholds before surfacing "N views" to avoid re-identifying a single unique viewer) — consider whether showing an exact count of "1 view" is itself information-leaking to the patient in a way worth guarding against (e.g., only surface counts above a small threshold, or bucket into ranges).
- Time-boxed/capability-URL design patterns (similar to signed S3 URLs or short-lived share links in consumer products) for the scoped-token scheme — the key property to replicate is unguessability plus a server-side-enforced, tamper-proof expiry/use-count, not merely an obscure-but-permanent URL.
- Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023's provisions on data-subject awareness of processing/access, as the compliance motivation for the access-log half of this issue.
